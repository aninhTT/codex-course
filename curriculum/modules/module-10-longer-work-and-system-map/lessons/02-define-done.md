# Lesson 10.02: Write a Goal you can verify

Describe the result clearly enough that someone could inspect it and decide whether it is done.

## Learn

A useful Goal names an **outcome**, **constraints**, and **verification**. Verification is the evidence that proves the requested result exists.

Sketch the output before writing the Goal. For a source audit, the output might be a table of sources, affected copies, dates, and unresolved questions. That makes the finish line concrete.

Limit the allowed sources and actions. State what the assistant should report if an input is missing. These are practical writing prompts, not a required sentence formula.

## See It

“Create a table of every output in my workflow map, its source, and whether I verified it. Use only listed course materials. Identify missing evidence instead of guessing.”

## Practice

Your tutor guides one step at a time in Work or Codex. Continue the recommended team-update example, or adapt the steps to your saved workflow choice.

1. Sketch the document, table, or other result your longer task should produce.

2. Write a Goal that names that result, its required evidence, and the sources it may use.

3. Add the boundaries and missing-input behavior needed for this task.

4. Ask the tutor to identify vague or unobservable wording. Tighten the draft and save it with the output sketch.

**Success criteria:** Your saved Goal names a visible result, checkable evidence, and clear boundaries.

## Challenge — Optional

Keep a vague Goal beside your revised one and annotate exactly what makes the revision testable.

**Success criteria:** Each improvement adds observable evidence or a useful limit.

## Quick Check

What did the assistant assume? Which fact or behavior did you verify yourself?

## Carry Forward

Keep the Goal draft, output sketch, constraints, and evidence criteria.
