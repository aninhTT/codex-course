# Lesson 10.05: Keep the useful parts and improve one thing

Finish with a workflow you can maintain, not a collection of forgotten experiments.

## Learn

A **workflow map** connects sources, methods, tools, review, and outputs. It helps you see missing ownership, duplicate copies, and schedules nobody checks.

Keep the pieces that serve a real task. Improve one weak instruction or handoff from observed use. Retire practice schedules when you no longer need them.

Work and Codex remain available after the course. Choose the surface for the task rather than treating one as a required upgrade from the other.

## See It

A weekly review of the draft and a monthly source check may be enough. You do not need a new automation for every part of the workflow.

## Practice

Your tutor guides one step at a time in Work or Codex. Continue the recommended team-update example, or adapt the steps to your saved workflow choice.

1. Open your workflow map and locate the actual sources, skills, schedule, reviewed outputs, and any prototype or Goal.

2. Ask for a review of gaps and duplication. Choose one small improvement based on evidence.

3. Make and test that improvement. Record who owns the active workflow and when they review it.

4. Keep, pause, or delete practice tasks deliberately. Ask for a closing recap and one real next use case.

**Success criteria:** Your final map shows what is active, who owns it, and how they review it. It includes one tested improvement.

## Challenge — Optional

Use the workflow on a real task after the course and add one lesson learned to the skill or project instructions.

**Success criteria:** The reusable method improves from experience rather than accumulating speculative rules.

## Quick Check

What did the assistant assume? Which fact or behavior did you verify yourself?

## Carry Forward

Save the final map, tested improvement, schedule decisions, and closing recap.
