# Lesson 1.03: Find the sources and ask a better question

A useful assistant still needs the right information. Let’s see what it can actually read.

## Learn

A **source** is information used to do the work: a document, meeting note, spreadsheet, or file. Giving the assistant a title does not guarantee it can open the source.

Ask it to show the information it found before drafting a larger result. This catches the wrong week, an inaccessible document, or a missing section early.

Keep unknown details visible. A confident sentence about an invented owner creates more work for your team. If you use real material, follow the company’s AI Expectations.

## See It

The sample says someone needs to review open questions. It names no owner. A good draft flags “Owner needed” instead of assigning Maya.

## Practice

Your tutor guides one step at a time in Work or Codex. Continue the recommended team-update example, or adapt the steps to your saved workflow choice.

1. Choose one source for your task. Use a short work document or the sample week-01-notes.md.

2. Ask the assistant to summarize three facts and name the source it read. Help it attach or open the file if needed.

3. Check one fact yourself. Point out any difference between the source and the summary.

4. Ask it to list information the source does not provide. Keep those questions for the next draft.

**Success criteria:** You verified one fact and identified at least one missing detail without filling it by guesswork.

## Challenge — Optional

Give the assistant two notes that disagree. Ask it to show the disagreement and explain what you need to resolve.

**Success criteria:** The result preserves the disagreement rather than silently choosing a convenient answer.

## Quick Check

What did the assistant assume? Which fact or behavior did you verify yourself?

## Carry Forward

Keep your verified source, checked fact, and questions about missing information.
