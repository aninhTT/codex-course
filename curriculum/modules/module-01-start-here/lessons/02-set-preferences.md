# Lesson 1.02: Make the assistant easier to work with

Give the assistant the same working preferences you would give a new colleague.

## Learn

**Personal instructions** describe how you want help across chats. **Project instructions** apply to one body of work. **Memory**, where available, can carry useful context forward.

Start with preferences you can recognize in the result: short explanations, plain language, or a summary of changes. “Be helpful” is harder to check than “explain unfamiliar terms before using them.”

Style preferences do not grant access to more tools or files. Keep required workflow rules in project instructions or a skill rather than relying only on memory.

## See It

“Use plain language. Explain unfamiliar terms. When you change my work, show what changed and what I should review.”

## Practice

Your tutor guides one step at a time in Work or Codex. Continue the recommended team-update example, or adapt the steps to your saved workflow choice.

1. Read the example and choose two preferences that would help you.

2. Ask the assistant to rewrite the preferences in your own words. Remove anything you would not want in every conversation.

3. Open the personal-instruction settings in your chosen surface. The tutor can help find the current control. Save the preferences you chose.

4. Ask for another version of your first draft. Check whether the assistant followed your preferences.

**Success criteria:** You saved or deliberately kept a reusable instruction and tested its effect on one result.

## Challenge — Optional

Ask for the same explanation for a beginner and an expert. Choose which instruction best matches your needs.

**Success criteria:** You can identify a specific difference caused by the audience instruction.

## Quick Check

What did the assistant assume? Which fact or behavior did you verify yourself?

## Carry Forward

Keep the preference you chose and the result you used to test it.
