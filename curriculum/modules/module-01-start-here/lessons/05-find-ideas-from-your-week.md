# Lesson 1.05: Find AI ideas from your actual week

You have connected your tools. Now let the assistant help you spot work it could take off your plate.

## Learn

You do not need to arrive with a list of AI use cases. Your calendar, meeting notes, and project activity can provide a starting point.

Look for patterns: repeated meetings, scattered action items, recurring status updates, or information you keep gathering by hand. A pattern suggests a possible task; it does not prove that automating it will help.

For this activity, use your calendar, Granola notes, and Linear project activity from the last seven days. Ask the assistant to state the dates and your timezone. If you mean the previous calendar week, say so.

The recommended course example is a weekly team update. It connects naturally to skills, recording, schedules, Pages, and a Site. You can use it, or choose another useful task. Once you choose, carry that same workflow into the next modules.

## See It

Try a request like this in Work or Codex:

> Look through my calendar, Granola notes, and the Linear projects I work on from the last seven days. State the dates you will use. Based on what you actually find, suggest three to five useful tasks you could help me with. For each, explain the pattern you noticed, link to the relevant source, describe the result you could produce, and give me a starter request. Recommend one easy first task. Review and suggest only; do not change records, send messages, or create schedules.

An illustrative pattern might be three project meetings with decisions scattered across notes. A useful suggestion is a weekly update that gathers decisions, blockers, and next steps. The assistant should show the retrieved evidence before making that recommendation about your week.

## Practice

Your tutor will guide one step at a time. You can stay in Work or Codex.

1. Confirm the date range and sources. Use the connections from Lesson 1.04: Google Calendar, Granola, and Linear. Ask for setup help if one still needs sign-in.

2. Run the request above. Focus on your meetings, your accessible notes, and projects you work on. Have the assistant distinguish retrieved activity from suggestions or inferences.

3. Read the three to five ideas. Check one source and ask: “Would this result save me time or make my work better?” Set aside ideas that do neither.

4. Choose the recommended weekly team update or another task you prefer. Save the choice, audience, source links, and expected result as a short opportunity note for Module 2.

**Success criteria:** You reviewed source-backed suggestions and chose one workflow to carry through the course. Record any source the assistant could not inspect.

## Challenge — Optional

Try a small, read-only version of your favorite idea. For example, ask for a five-bullet catch-up brief from one meeting and its related project activity.

**Success criteria:** You tested an idea on real information and can explain whether the result was useful.

## Quick Check

Which suggestion came from something the assistant actually found? Which part still depends on your judgment?

## Carry Forward

Bring your chosen workflow, audience, sources, and expected output into Module 2. If you are unsure, follow the weekly team-update example.

## Tutor Notes

Use real retrieval when the learner requests the activity. Do not invent meeting patterns or claim you checked a source you could not access. If Linear does not expose historical activity, explain that limitation rather than describing current status as last week's activity.

If sources are unavailable or the learner prefers not to use personal work information, use the fictional course notes. Label those as sample-based ideas and continue. Do not block the course on connecting every service.

Recommend the team-update path without requiring it. Save the learner's choice and use it consistently in later activities. Reading this lesson during course authoring does not authorize searching the author's accounts.
