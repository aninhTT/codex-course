# Lesson 2.03: Create your first useful update

Use your sources to make something you could actually use this week.

## Learn

A strong task names the **outcome**, **sources**, **time period**, and **output format**. “Catch me up” leaves much more to guess than a request for decisions and action items since Monday.

Your task might be a weekly update, meeting brief, project summary, or research digest. Start with the version your reader needs. Keep supporting evidence separately when the final update should be short.

The sample week has missing owners and dates on purpose. Ask for questions alongside the draft. Save this first version so Clean Draft can improve it later.

## See It

“Use my project notes from this week to prepare a short update for team leads. Lead with the decision needed, then progress, blockers, and next steps. Flag missing details separately.”

## Practice

Your tutor guides one step at a time in Work or Codex. Continue the recommended team-update example, or adapt the steps to your saved workflow choice.

1. Choose your real source material or the sample notes and update template.

2. Write your own request using the four ingredients above. Name the reader and the result they need.

3. Run the task here in your chosen surface. Save the original draft in your project or practice outputs.

4. Open it and check one claim against a source. Keep unresolved questions with the draft.

**Success criteria:** You created and saved a first draft with traceable sources and visible gaps.

## Challenge — Optional

Ask for a second version half as long. Decide which version better serves the reader and identify anything important it cut.

**Success criteria:** You choose a version based on the reader’s needs, not just its length.

## Quick Check

What did the assistant assume? Which fact or behavior did you verify yourself?

## Carry Forward

Keep the original draft and its source list for review and Clean Draft.
