# Lesson 2.02: Give the assistant a short work briefing

Tell it enough about your role and audience to make the result useful.

## Learn

**Context** is the background that changes what a good answer looks like. A new colleague needs to know who reads the update, what decisions matter, and which sources are reliable.

Create a short workflow brief with your role, audience, recurring task, sources, and biggest frustration. Then keep the most important rules in project instructions.

This brief is reusable. It will help you choose a skill, judge a draft, and explain the workflow in a new chat. Keep personal information limited to what the work needs.

## See It

“I prepare a Friday update for team leads. They need decisions, blockers, and next steps. Use our meeting notes; flag missing owners and dates.”

## Practice

Your tutor guides one step at a time in Work or Codex. Continue the recommended team-update example, or adapt the steps to your saved workflow choice.

1. Tell the tutor your role, audience, and one recurring task. Voice dictation is fine if talking is easier.

2. Ask it to create my-workflow-brief.md in your practice outputs or an equivalent project document.

3. Add two or three useful rules to the project instructions. Review the proposed wording before saving.

4. Ask the assistant what result it plans to produce from this context. Correct one assumption.

**Success criteria:** Your saved brief names the audience, sources, outcome, and one useful rule.

## Challenge — Optional

Describe three recurring tasks from your job. Ask which one would be most useful to turn into a skill and why.

**Success criteria:** You choose a small repeatable task with a result you can review.

## Quick Check

What did the assistant assume? Which fact or behavior did you verify yourself?

## Carry Forward

Bring your saved workflow brief and the project rules you reviewed.
