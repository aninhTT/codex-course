# Lesson 7.02: Turn a message into a small presentation

Use slides when you need to guide a conversation in a meeting.

## Learn

A **presentation** gives a room a sequence of points to discuss. Each slide needs a clear purpose. A document’s paragraphs rarely make a useful deck when pasted unchanged.

Start with three slides: what changed, what needs a decision, and what happens next. Use the facts from your reviewed workflow. If your audience needs another structure, explain it.

Ask for a focused revision by slide or element. Use an approved template when you have one. Keep charts tied to real data rather than asking for decoration that looks quantitative.

## See It

“Create three slides for our team meeting from this update. Keep one main point per slide and make the decision request clear.”

## Practice

Your tutor guides one step at a time in Work or Codex. Continue the recommended team-update example, or adapt the steps to your saved workflow choice.

1. Name the meeting and the decision or discussion the deck should support.

2. Create a small deck from your reviewed sources. Supply an approved template if you want it followed.

3. View the slides at presentation size. Cut text from one slide yourself, then ask the assistant for another focused edit.

4. Compare the final claims with the sources and save or export the deck where you will present it.

**Success criteria:** You created and revised a readable small deck with a clear purpose and checked facts.

## Challenge — Optional

Compare the deck with your document. Choose the one this audience should receive rather than sending both by default.

**Success criteria:** You can defend the format choice and identify what changed when the content became slides.

## Quick Check

What did the assistant assume? Which fact or behavior did you verify yourself?

## Carry Forward

Keep the revised deck and any decision about using it instead of a document.
