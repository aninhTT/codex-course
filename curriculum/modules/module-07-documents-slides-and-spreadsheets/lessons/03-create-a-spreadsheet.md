# Lesson 7.03: Build a spreadsheet you can check

Turn a few numbers into a useful comparison, then verify the calculation yourself.

## Learn

A **spreadsheet** organizes rows and calculations. A **formula** calculates a result from cells. Ask the assistant to explain a formula if you cannot tell what it means.

Use your own suitable data or `sample-metrics.csv`. The fictional sample has 20 requests received in each week. Completed requests rise from 16 to 17. That is one more completed request, not evidence of a cause.

Create the table and calculation before the chart. Clear labels and honest units matter more than a polished style. Keep the source unchanged.

## See It

“Create a spreadsheet from this CSV with weekly totals and the change in completed requests. Explain the formula and add one simple chart.”

## Practice

Your tutor guides one step at a time in Work or Codex. Continue the recommended team-update example, or adapt the steps to your saved workflow choice.

1. Choose a small data file and identify its rows, columns, and what they represent.

2. Ask the assistant to create a local spreadsheet or Google Sheet with the source rows, weekly totals, and the change between periods.

3. Check one total yourself and inspect the formula. Correct any problem before adding a chart.

4. Add a simple chart, compare its labels with the table, and save the reviewed file. Label course data fictional.

**Success criteria:** You verified a total and a formula, then checked that the chart matches the data.

## Challenge — Optional

Introduce a missing value in a separate practice copy. Ask the assistant to flag it and explain its effect on the result.

**Success criteria:** The result distinguishes missing data from zero and preserves the original source.

## Quick Check

What did the assistant assume? Which fact or behavior did you verify yourself?

## Carry Forward

Keep the checked spreadsheet, formula explanation, and source data.
