# Lesson 7.01: Make a document for a real reader

Create the brief or report your audience needs, then cut what they do not need.

## Learn

A **document** works well when someone will read a brief, proposal, or report on their own. Decide what they need to know or do before choosing the format.

Use a Page, an exported work file, or a Google Doc according to where your audience works. The assistant can help create and revise it in Work or Codex.

Own the result before sharing it: verify the facts, make the ask easy to find, and remove unnecessary background. Clean Draft can help with the writing. Its output still needs your judgment.

## See It

“Turn this reviewed update into a one-page brief for my manager. Lead with the decision needed, then reasons, risks, and next steps.”

## Practice

Your tutor guides one step at a time in Work or Codex. Continue the recommended team-update example, or adapt the steps to your saved workflow choice.

1. Turn the reviewed team update into a one-page brief for team leads. If you chose another workflow, use its reviewed result for the same audience exercise.

2. Choose a Page, Google Doc, or downloadable document and ask the assistant to create it from your reviewed sources.

3. Open it. Cut one unnecessary paragraph or section and make the request easy to find. Use Clean Draft if helpful.

4. Check the facts and layout, then save the version in the destination your reader expects.

**Success criteria:** You created a document for a named reader, improved it, and checked the actual saved result.

## Challenge — Optional

Get a reader’s feedback—or ask for a skeptical-reader review—and resolve two specific comments.

**Success criteria:** You can explain why the final document is ready for its intended audience.

## Quick Check

What did the assistant assume? Which fact or behavior did you verify yourself?

## Carry Forward

Keep the reviewed document, sources, audience, and destination.
