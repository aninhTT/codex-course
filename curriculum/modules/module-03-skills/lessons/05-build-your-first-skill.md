# Lesson 3.05: Build your first workflow skill

Turn the work you have already done into a method you can reuse next week.

## Learn

Your first custom skill should solve one repeated problem. Keep the inputs, steps, and output small enough to test in a few minutes.

The recommended skill, weekly-update-helper, reads the requested period’s notes, follows the update template, flags missing owners and dates, and saves a draft. It is the same method you will connect to recording, scheduling, a Page, and a Site.

Review the skill before finalizing it. Use Clean Draft as a separate writing-quality check. Once both methods work, you can try them together. Preparing a draft does not need to include sending or publishing it.

## See It

“Create weekly-update-helper from our plan. Use my notes and template, preserve facts, flag gaps, and save a draft. Show me the instructions before finalizing.”

## Practice

Your tutor guides one step at a time in Work or Codex. Continue the recommended team-update example, or adapt the steps to your saved workflow choice.

1. Build weekly-update-helper from your Skill Creator plan. If you chose another workflow, build its corresponding skill and keep that same choice for later modules.

2. Ask the creator to draft the skill in a separate practice folder. Review its inputs, numbered steps, output, and missing-source behavior.

3. Correct a rule that does not match your real workflow. Finalize the skill and confirm how the assistant will find it.

4. Run it with one realistic input. Open the result and check it against your original task.

**Success criteria:** You built a focused skill, reviewed its instructions, and tested it once.

## Challenge — Optional

Add a clearly defined preference, such as a short executive summary. Rerun the skill and compare the result.

**Success criteria:** The added rule changes the intended part without disrupting the rest of the method.

## Quick Check

What did the assistant assume? Which fact or behavior did you verify yourself?

## Carry Forward

Keep your new skill, its invocation method, and the result of its first test.
