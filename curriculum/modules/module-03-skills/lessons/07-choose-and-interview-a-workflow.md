# Lesson 3.07: Choose And Interview A Workflow

## Where You Are

You built a first practical skill. Now you will choose a workflow from your use-case shortlist or your own work that might benefit from a more custom skill.

## Learn

Do not jump to the first workflow that comes to mind. A good beginner workflow is repetitive, clear enough to describe, small enough to test, and useful enough that saving time would matter.

Codex should ask several narrowing questions before recommending what to build:

- What do you do regularly?
- What feels slow, repetitive, annoying, or easy to forget?
- What inputs do you usually start with?
- What output do you need?
- Who reviews or uses the output?
- What makes the work good or bad?
- What should the first version leave out?

If talking is faster than typing, use voice dictation. Messy spoken context is often enough for Codex to help organize the workflow.

Screenshot slot: `curriculum/assets/screenshots/voice-dictation-entrypoint.png`

## Practice

Try this in Codex:

```text
Help me choose one workflow to turn into a custom skill. Start from `learner-assets/use-case-shortlist.md` if it has useful candidates. Interview me with several short questions before recommending anything. Ask about my role, recurring work, pain points, inputs, desired output, review criteria, edge cases, and what to leave out of the first version. Encourage me to use voice dictation if talking through the context would be faster. End with 2 or 3 candidate workflows, a recommendation, and a concise workflow summary we can build from.
```

**Success criteria:** The learner ends with one clear workflow summary that is specific enough to build from.

## What You Made Or Decided

You chose a workflow based on a real pain point, not just the first idea that appeared.
