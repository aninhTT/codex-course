# Lesson 8.03: Choose who can visit and edit

Make the audience an explicit part of the product you built.

## Learn

**Visitor access** controls who can open a Site. **Editor access** controls who can change it. A sign-in feature inside an app is separate from the Site’s audience setting.

Choose the narrowest audience that serves the task. A production URL can still have limited access. Test the visitor experience rather than assuming a link works for everyone.

For the sample hub, storage is unnecessary. If your real workflow needs saved records, describe what the Site saves and who may read or change it. Keep secret values out of its source.

## See It

“Teammates should read the hub. One maintainer should edit it. Visitors should not be able to change the displayed update.”

## Practice

Your tutor guides one step at a time in Work or Codex. Continue the recommended team-update example, or adapt the steps to your saved workflow choice.

1. Inspect the Site’s available audience and editing controls.

2. Choose an intended audience and maintainer. Distinguish the people who read it from those who change it.

3. Review visible content, source links, any forms, and any stored data for that audience.

4. Record the access choice and resolve blockers before deployment. Do not widen access just to make testing easier.

**Success criteria:** You chose an audience and editor role and checked the Site’s data needs.

## Challenge — Optional

Write down how you would remove a visitor or editor and verify that inherited or broader access does not still apply.

**Success criteria:** Your access-removal plan checks the actual routes by which someone can reach the Site.

## Quick Check

What did the assistant assume? Which fact or behavior did you verify yourself?

## Carry Forward

Bring the audience choice, editor role, and resolved readiness checks to deployment.
